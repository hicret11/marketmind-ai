## Definition

The sales funnel is the specific sequence a lead moves through once identified — distinct from the broader marketing funnel — typically covering qualification, proposal, negotiation and close, especially relevant for higher-touch or B2B sales.

## Why it matters

Without a defined sales funnel, leads sit in an unclear state — "maybe interested" — with no consistent process to move them toward a decision, causing good leads to go cold from neglect rather than genuine disinterest.

:::key-idea
A sales funnel's job is to make "what happens next" unambiguous at every stage, for both the seller and the prospect.
:::

## How it works

A common structure:

1. **Lead** — identified, not yet qualified.
2. **Qualified** — confirmed fit and some level of interest.
3. **Proposal/Pilot offered** — a concrete next step is on the table.
4. **Negotiation** — details, pricing or scope being finalized.
5. **Closed (won/lost)** — a clear outcome, tracked either way.

## Framework

1. Define clear criteria for what moves a lead from one stage to the next.
2. Set a maximum time a lead can sit in a stage before requiring a follow-up action.
3. Track lost deals with a reason, not just "didn't convert" — reasons reveal fixable patterns.
4. Review the funnel weekly for anything stalled.

:::example
A venue partnership funnel: outreach sent → replied/qualified → pilot proposed → pilot running → converted to ongoing partnership. Each stage has an obvious next action.
:::

## Common mistakes

- No defined stages — leads exist only as "interested" with no structure.
- Letting leads go stale with no scheduled follow-up.
- Not tracking lost-deal reasons, missing patterns like "price objection" or "bad timing."

:::mistake
Treating every lead as equally likely to close and spending equal time on all of them instead of prioritizing by fit and stage.
:::

## Metrics to track

- Conversion rate stage-to-stage
- Average time spent per stage
- Win rate and most common loss reasons
