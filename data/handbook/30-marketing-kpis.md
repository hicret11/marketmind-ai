## Definition

A Key Performance Indicator (KPI) is a specific, measurable metric chosen because it meaningfully reflects progress toward a business goal — not just any number that's easy to measure.

## Why it matters

Businesses that track everything often act on nothing, because no single number is clearly "the one that matters this month." A small, well-chosen KPI set focuses attention and makes trade-offs clearer.

:::key-idea
A good KPI is specific, tied to a real goal, and something the team can actually influence with their actions — vague or uncontrollable metrics don't qualify.
:::

## How it works

KPIs typically span:

- **Acquisition** — new leads, new customers, cost per acquisition.
- **Engagement** — activation rate, content engagement.
- **Revenue** — sales, average order value, ROAS.
- **Retention** — repeat rate, churn, referral rate.

Choose a handful (3–5) that reflect the current stage and priority of the business — not every metric available.

## Framework

1. Start from the current business goal (growth, efficiency, retention) and pick KPIs that map directly to it.
2. Set a target and a review cadence for each KPI.
3. Avoid changing KPIs too often — consistency lets you see real trends.
4. Revisit the KPI set when the business stage or priority shifts (e.g. from "get first customers" to "improve retention").

:::example
An early-stage focus for Sing My Birthday's B2B arm might be: qualified conversations per week, pilot conversion rate, and pilots-to-paid-partnership rate — not broad brand awareness metrics yet.
:::

## Common mistakes

- Tracking 20+ metrics with no prioritization, diluting focus.
- Choosing KPIs that are easy to measure but not actually tied to the business goal.
- Changing the KPI set every month, making trends impossible to read.

:::mistake
Optimizing hard for a KPI that's a proxy (e.g. website traffic) while the real goal (revenue, bookings) stays flat or even declines.
:::

## Metrics to track

- The chosen KPI set itself, reviewed on a fixed cadence
- Leading indicators that predict each KPI a few weeks ahead
- KPI trend over time, not just a single snapshot
