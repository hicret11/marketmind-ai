## Definition

Market research is the disciplined process of gathering real evidence about customers, competitors and demand — instead of guessing. It answers: does this problem exist, for whom, how badly, and who else is already solving it?

## Why it matters

Every marketing decision downstream (audience, positioning, channel, pricing) is only as good as the research behind it. Skipping research doesn't save time — it just moves the cost to later, when you find out the wrong way (empty pipeline, wasted ad spend).

:::key-idea
Research isn't a report you write once. It's a habit of checking assumptions against reality before committing budget to them.
:::

## How it works

Two broad types, both useful:

- **Qualitative** — customer interviews, sales call notes, support tickets, open-ended survey answers. Tells you *why*.
- **Quantitative** — search volume, market size estimates, pricing benchmarks, survey scores at scale. Tells you *how much* and *how many*.

## Framework

1. Talk to 5–10 real (or prospective) customers before writing a single ad.
2. Map 3–5 direct and indirect competitors: their pricing, positioning, and where their marketing is weak.
3. Size the opportunity roughly — is this a niche or a real market?
4. Write down the assumptions you're making, and mark which ones are still unverified.
5. Re-check assumptions every quarter — markets shift.

:::example
Before pitching venues, a quick scan of 15 local kids' party venues' Instagram and websites can reveal how many already advertise "personalization," what they charge, and what language they use — cheap, fast research that shapes the entire pitch.
:::

## Common mistakes

- Only researching to confirm what you already believe (confirmation bias).
- Relying solely on secondary data (reports, articles) with no direct customer contact.
- Treating one enthusiastic customer conversation as proof of a broad trend.

:::mistake
Skipping competitor research because "we're different." Even an indirect competitor (including a customer's "do nothing" alternative) shapes what your positioning has to overcome.
:::

## Metrics to track

- Number of direct customer conversations per month
- Search volume / interest trends for core terms
- Competitor pricing and offer changes over time
