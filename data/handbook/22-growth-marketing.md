## Definition

Growth marketing is a systematic, experiment-driven approach to finding and scaling the levers that most move a core business metric — combining marketing, product and data thinking rather than sticking to one channel or campaign type.

## Why it matters

Traditional marketing often focuses on execution within known channels. Growth marketing focuses on discovery — testing across the entire funnel (acquisition, activation, retention, referral) to find under-used, high-leverage opportunities.

:::key-idea
Growth marketing treats every part of the customer experience as a testable surface, not just ads — onboarding, pricing page, email timing, even the product itself.
:::

## How it works

A common frame is AARRR (Acquisition, Activation, Retention, Referral, Revenue) — identifying which stage has the most leverage right now rather than assuming it's always "get more traffic."

## Framework

1. Define one North Star metric that reflects real business value (not vanity reach).
2. Generate a backlog of testable hypotheses across the full funnel, not just acquisition.
3. Prioritize tests by potential impact × confidence × ease.
4. Run tests with a clear success metric and a fixed time box; document results either way.

:::example
Instead of only testing new ad creative, a growth approach might test whether adding a "hear a sample" audio preview on the landing page increases purchase conversion more than any new ad angle would.
:::

## Common mistakes

- Running growth "hacks" with no clear hypothesis or success metric.
- Only testing acquisition while retention or activation is the actual bottleneck.
- Declaring wins from tests with too little data to be meaningful.

:::mistake
Copying a tactic that worked for a different company without understanding why it worked or whether the same mechanism applies to your funnel.
:::

## Metrics to track

- North Star metric trend over time
- Experiment win rate and velocity (tests run per month)
- Funnel-stage conversion rates before/after each test
