## Definition

Marketing experimentation is the broader discipline of running structured tests — beyond simple A/B splits — across channels, messages, offers and audiences, treating marketing as a continuous set of hypotheses to validate rather than a fixed plan.

## Why it matters

Markets, platforms and audiences change constantly. A business that only executes a fixed plan without testing will slowly drift out of sync with what actually works, while competitors who experiment keep finding new efficiency.

:::key-idea
The goal of experimentation isn't to be "always testing" for its own sake — it's to systematically replace guesses with evidence on the decisions that matter most.
:::

## How it works

A simple experimentation system:

1. **Backlog** — a running list of hypotheses worth testing.
2. **Prioritization** — rank by potential impact, confidence, and ease of running.
3. **Execution** — run with a clear metric and time box.
4. **Documentation** — record what was tested, the result, and what it implies going forward.

## Framework

- Keep a shared, living document of hypotheses — don't rely on memory.
- Prioritize tests that could meaningfully change strategy, not just minor tweaks.
- Set a cadence (e.g. one meaningful test running at all times) rather than testing sporadically.
- Review past tests before starting new ones — avoid re-testing something already answered.

:::example
A quarterly experimentation backlog for Sing My Birthday might include: testing gift-occasion messaging vs. self-purchase messaging, testing a video sample vs. audio-only sample on the landing page, and testing a B2B pilot offer with vs. without a defined trial period.
:::

## Common mistakes

- Running experiments with no documentation, so learnings are lost or repeated.
- Testing only low-impact details while ignoring bigger strategic uncertainties.
- No prioritization system, so whichever idea is loudest gets tested next.

:::mistake
Treating a single test result as a permanent truth rather than one data point that may need re-validation as the market or audience shifts.
:::

## Metrics to track

- Number of experiments run per period (velocity)
- Percentage of experiments that produced an actionable learning
- Compounding impact of implemented winners over time
