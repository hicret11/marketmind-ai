## Definition

Creative testing is systematically comparing different ad creative — hooks, formats, angles, visuals — to find what actually earns attention and drives results, before committing significant budget to any one version.

## Why it matters

On most modern ad platforms, creative quality is now a larger performance lever than targeting precision. A weak creative wastes an otherwise well-targeted, well-budgeted campaign.

:::key-idea
Test angles (the core message/emotion) before polishing production value — a rough version of the right angle usually beats a polished version of the wrong one.
:::

## How it works

Dimensions worth testing:

- **Hook** — the first 1–3 seconds/first line that earns attention.
- **Angle** — the core emotional or logical argument (e.g. "unique gift" vs. "make them cry happy tears").
- **Format** — video vs. static vs. carousel, UGC-style vs. produced.
- **Offer** — the specific promise or incentive presented.

## Framework

1. Test angles first with cheap, rough creative before investing in production.
2. Isolate one dimension per test round where possible (hook, then format, then offer).
3. Let the data pick the winner — don't personally "like" a version into staying alive.
4. Refresh winning creative periodically to fight fatigue, using the same proven angle in new executions.

:::example
Testing a "reaction video" angle against a "here's exactly what you get" explainer angle reveals which emotional register actually drives more clicks and purchases for a given audience — a bigger lever than testing five thumbnail colors of the same video.
:::

## Common mistakes

- Only testing minor visual tweaks (colors, fonts) instead of fundamentally different angles.
- Keeping underperforming creative alive due to personal preference.
- Never refreshing a winning ad, letting it fatigue an audience over time.

:::mistake
Judging creative performance on too small an audience or too short a time window to see a reliable signal.
:::

## Metrics to track

- Hook rate / thumb-stop rate (early engagement)
- Cost per result by creative variant
- Frequency and performance decay over time (fatigue signal)
