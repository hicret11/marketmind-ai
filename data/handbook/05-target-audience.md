## Definition

Your target audience is the specific group of people (or businesses) your marketing is written for right now — specific enough that you could picture one real person or company and write directly to them.

## Why it matters

Marketing that tries to speak to everyone ends up speaking clearly to no one. A sharply defined audience lets every headline, image and offer feel personally relevant instead of generic.

:::key-idea
If your ideal customer wouldn't say "this is for me" within 3 seconds of seeing your ad or landing page, the audience definition isn't sharp enough yet.
:::

## How it works

A useful target audience definition includes:

- **Who** — role, life stage, or business type
- **Context** — what's happening in their life/business right now that creates the need
- **Current alternative** — what they do today instead of using you
- **Objection** — the main reason they'd hesitate to buy

## Framework

1. Start from a real customer (or ideal-fit prospect), not an invented composite.
2. Write one paragraph describing their situation in their own likely words.
3. Identify the trigger moment — the event that makes them start looking for a solution.
4. Test the definition: could you write an ad headline that this specific person would stop scrolling for?

:::example
Not: "Parents who love their kids." Instead: "A parent organizing their child's birthday party in the next month, who wants the party to feel special and is already looking at venues or party add-ons online."
:::

## Common mistakes

- Defining an audience so broad it doesn't change any marketing decisions.
- Building the audience definition around who you *wish* bought, not who actually does.
- Never revisiting the definition as real customer data comes in.

:::mistake
Assuming your target audience for a B2C product (parents) is the same audience you need for the B2B side of the same product (venue owners). They need entirely separate audience definitions.
:::

## Metrics to track

- Match rate: how well ad clicks/leads resemble the defined audience
- Message resonance (engagement rate, reply rate, save rate) within that audience
- Cost per qualified lead within the target segment vs. outside it
