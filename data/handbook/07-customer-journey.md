## Definition

The customer journey is the full sequence of stages and touchpoints a person moves through — from first becoming aware of a problem, to considering options, to buying, to using the product, to (ideally) buying again or referring others.

## Why it matters

Most marketing only optimizes one stage (usually the ad that drives the first click) while ignoring the rest of the path. A broken journey — a confusing landing page, a slow follow-up, an awkward checkout — can waste all the value the earlier marketing created.

:::key-idea
A customer journey map isn't decoration — it's a diagnostic tool for finding exactly where prospects are dropping off.
:::

## How it works

A typical journey has five stages:

1. **Awareness** — they notice the problem or your brand.
2. **Consideration** — they compare options, including you.
3. **Decision** — they choose (or don't).
4. **Onboarding/Delivery** — they experience the product for the first time.
5. **Loyalty/Advocacy** — they return, upgrade, or refer others.

## Framework

1. List every touchpoint a real customer hits at each stage (ad, website, DM, email, checkout, delivery, follow-up).
2. For each touchpoint, note the goal, the emotion the customer likely feels, and any friction.
3. Identify the single highest-friction point — that's usually where the biggest win is, not a new top-of-funnel channel.
4. Fix that friction before adding more traffic to a leaky journey.

:::example
For Sing My Birthday's B2C buyer: sees an ad → visits a landing page → fills in the child's name/story → pays → waits for delivery → receives the song → shares it. If "waits for delivery" has no status update, anxiety (and refund requests) go up — a journey fix, not an ad fix.
:::

## Common mistakes

- Mapping the journey you *wish* existed instead of what customers actually experience.
- Treating awareness and conversion as separate teams' problems instead of one connected system.
- Ignoring the post-purchase journey, which drives repeat business and referrals.

:::mistake
Pouring more ad budget into the top of the funnel when the real leak is in the middle (e.g. a confusing checkout) — this just makes the leak more expensive.
:::

## Metrics to track

- Drop-off rate at each journey stage
- Time between stages (e.g. first visit to purchase)
- Repeat purchase / referral rate as a signal of a healthy post-purchase journey
