## Definition

A landing page is a focused, single-purpose page designed to convert visitors coming from a specific source (an ad, an email, a link) into a specific action — distinct from a general website page trying to serve many purposes at once.

## Why it matters

Sending paid or high-intent traffic to a generic homepage dilutes conversion, because homepages try to serve many different visitor intents at once. A matched landing page removes everything that isn't relevant to that one visitor's context.

:::key-idea
Message match is the single biggest landing page lever: the page should continue the exact promise made in the ad or link that brought the visitor there.
:::

## How it works

A strong landing page typically includes, in order:

1. A headline that matches the ad/offer that drove the click.
2. A clear value proposition and, if visual, a strong hero image/video.
3. Proof (testimonials, examples, results).
4. A single, unambiguous call to action, repeated as needed.
5. Objection handling (FAQ, guarantee) near the decision point.

## Framework

1. Build one landing page per distinct offer/audience — don't reuse one page for every campaign.
2. Match headline and imagery directly to the referring ad or link.
3. Remove navigation and distractions that let visitors leave without converting.
4. Keep the form/action as short as it can be while still qualifying the lead.

:::example
An ad promising "hear a sample personalized song" should land on a page that plays a sample immediately — not a generic homepage the visitor has to search through.
:::

## Common mistakes

- Sending all traffic to the homepage regardless of the ad's specific promise.
- Cluttering the page with full site navigation, giving visitors an easy way to wander off.
- A call-to-action that's vague ("Learn More") instead of specific ("Hear Your Sample").

:::mistake
Making the form longer than necessary "just in case" — every extra field measurably reduces completion rate.
:::

## Metrics to track

- Conversion rate by traffic source
- Bounce rate and time-to-first-interaction
- Page load speed on mobile specifically
