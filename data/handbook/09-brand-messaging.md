## Definition

A brand is the set of associations and trust a person has with your business, built up over every interaction. Messaging is the specific language you use to express that brand consistently across channels.

## Why it matters

Consistent messaging compounds — every touchpoint reinforces the same promise, making the brand easier to recognize and trust. Inconsistent messaging forces customers to re-learn who you are every time, which slows trust and conversion.

:::key-idea
Brand is what people say about you when you're not in the room. Messaging is your best attempt to influence that.
:::

## How it works

A workable messaging framework covers:

- **Core promise** — the one outcome you consistently deliver.
- **Tone of voice** — how you sound (warm, playful, premium, direct) across every channel.
- **Proof points** — the evidence that backs the promise (reviews, results, guarantees).
- **Key phrases** — a small set of recurring lines used across ads, site copy and social captions.

## Framework

1. Write the core promise in one sentence, in plain language a customer would use.
2. Define 3 tone-of-voice adjectives (e.g. "warm, personal, premium — not cutesy or corporate").
3. Build a short messaging doc: promise, proof points, tone, and 3–5 reusable phrases.
4. Check every new piece of marketing content against that doc before publishing.

:::example
Sing My Birthday's core promise might be "a birthday moment that feels made just for them" — with a warm, personal, slightly premium tone, avoiding generic "fun for the whole family" language that doesn't differentiate.
:::

## Common mistakes

- Changing tone and message every campaign instead of building one recognizable voice.
- Writing messaging that describes the company instead of the customer's outcome.
- Letting every team member write ad copy without a shared reference doc.

:::mistake
Sounding like every competitor because the copy leans on generic category language ("premium," "personalized," "the best") without specifics that are actually true only of you.
:::

## Metrics to track

- Brand recall / recognition (surveys, direct traffic, branded search)
- Message consistency across channels (a simple audit, not a hard number)
- Sentiment in reviews and comments
