## Definition

- **CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)** — total spend to acquire one customer (marketing + sales cost ÷ customers acquired).
- **LTV (Lifetime Value)** — the total revenue (or profit) a customer generates over their relationship with the business.
- **ROAS (Return on Ad Spend)** — revenue generated per dollar of ad spend (revenue ÷ ad spend).
- **ROI (Return on Investment)** — overall return relative to total cost, including non-ad costs ((return − cost) ÷ cost).

## Why it matters

These four numbers together tell you whether marketing is actually making the business money, not just generating activity. A campaign can have a great ROAS and still be a bad idea if CAC is close to or above LTV.

:::key-idea
ROAS looks at ad spend alone; CAC vs. LTV looks at whether the whole business model works. A healthy business usually needs LTV to be several times CAC, not just barely above it.
:::

## How it works

Use them together:

- **When should you use ROAS?** For evaluating a specific campaign's immediate advertising efficiency, especially in e-commerce with a clear single-purchase revenue number.
- **When should you use CAC/LTV?** For evaluating whether the overall growth strategy is sustainable, especially when there's repeat purchase or long customer relationships involved.
- **ROI** is the broadest view, useful when comparing marketing investment against other uses of the same budget.

## Framework

1. Calculate CAC by channel, not just in aggregate — channels vary widely in efficiency.
2. Estimate LTV even roughly for early-stage businesses — a rough number beats none.
3. Set a target ROAS or CAC:LTV ratio before scaling spend on a channel.
4. Recalculate periodically — costs and behavior change as channels mature or saturate.

:::example
If Sing My Birthday spends $500 to acquire 10 customers via ads generating $50 each in first purchase (ROAS of 1.0), that alone looks weak — but if half of those customers buy again next year (raising LTV), the real picture is healthier than ROAS alone suggests.
:::

## Common mistakes

- Judging a channel on ROAS alone without factoring in repeat purchase/LTV.
- Never calculating CAC by channel, hiding which channels are actually efficient.
- Using an unrealistically optimistic LTV estimate to justify high CAC spending.

:::mistake
Chasing a headline ROAS number from a single campaign without accounting for returns, discounts, or the cost of goods/delivery — inflating apparent profitability.
:::

## Metrics to track

- CAC by channel and cohort
- LTV by segment/cohort, revisited as more repeat-purchase data comes in
- Blended ROAS/ROI across the whole marketing budget, not just individual campaigns
