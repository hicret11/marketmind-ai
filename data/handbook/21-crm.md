## Definition

CRM (Customer Relationship Management) is both the practice and the tooling used to track every interaction with a lead or customer — contact details, stage, history, notes — so follow-up is systematic rather than dependent on memory.

## Why it matters

Most lost deals aren't lost to competitors — they're lost to silence: a lead who was interested but never followed up with. A CRM turns "I think I talked to them" into a clear, trackable record.

:::key-idea
A CRM is only as useful as the discipline behind updating it — an accurate simple CRM beats a sophisticated one nobody keeps current.
:::

## How it works

A CRM record typically tracks:

- Contact and business information
- Current pipeline stage
- History of interactions (calls, emails, notes)
- Next scheduled action and owner

## Framework

1. Log every meaningful interaction immediately, not from memory later.
2. Always set a "next action + date" on every active record — nothing sits with no owner or deadline.
3. Review stalled records weekly (anything with no activity in X days).
4. Use the CRM to spot patterns (common objections, best-performing lead sources) — not just as a contact list.

:::example
For MarketMind's own Opportunity Discovery + CRM: every discovered venue with a strong fit score gets saved as a lead with its evidence and AI analysis attached, so outreach follow-up has full context instead of starting cold each time.
:::

## Common mistakes

- Using a CRM only as a static contact list, not as an active pipeline management tool.
- Letting records go stale with no next action defined.
- Duplicating records for the same contact/business across different tools.

:::mistake
Adding a lead to the CRM once and never updating its stage — the CRM becomes inaccurate and stops being trusted, so people stop using it.
:::

## Metrics to track

- Percentage of active leads with a defined next action
- Average days since last contact, across the pipeline
- Pipeline value by stage (even a rough estimate)
