## Definition

Lead generation is the process of identifying and capturing interest from potential customers — turning strangers into named contacts you can follow up with, rather than one-shot anonymous traffic.

## Why it matters

Not every valuable customer converts on the first visit or interaction, especially in B2B or higher-consideration purchases. Lead generation captures that interest so it isn't lost, and gives you a pipeline to manage instead of hoping for immediate conversions.

:::key-idea
A lead isn't a sale — it's a qualified conversation. The quality of the lead (how well it matches your ideal customer) matters more than raw volume.
:::

## How it works

Common lead sources:

- **Inbound** — content, SEO, ads that drive someone to opt in (form, download, call booking).
- **Outbound** — direct outreach to identified prospects (cold email, DMs, calls).
- **Referral/partnership** — leads sourced through existing customers or partner businesses.

For B2B specifically, this often means identifying real businesses that fit your ideal customer profile, then reaching out with a specific, relevant angle — not a generic pitch.

## Framework

1. Define what a "qualified" lead looks like before generating volume (fit + intent, not just contact info).
2. Choose lead sources matched to your sales motion — outbound for high-value B2B, inbound for scalable B2C.
3. Make the first ask small (a call, a reply) rather than a full commitment.
4. Follow up systematically — most value is lost in inconsistent or absent follow-up, not lead volume.

:::example
For Sing My Birthday's B2B side: identify venues that already run birthday packages (real evidence, e.g. website mentions of "party packages"), then reach out with a specific, evidenced observation rather than a generic "let's partner" message.
:::

## Common mistakes

- Optimizing purely for lead volume regardless of fit, overwhelming sales/follow-up capacity with poor-quality leads.
- No lead scoring or prioritization — treating every lead identically.
- Slow or inconsistent follow-up, especially on inbound leads where interest fades fast.

:::mistake
Sending generic, unpersonalized outreach at scale — response rates collapse when the prospect can tell it wasn't written with them specifically in mind.
:::

## Metrics to track

- Lead volume AND lead quality/fit rate (both matter)
- Lead-to-opportunity and opportunity-to-close conversion rate
- Time-to-first-response on new leads
