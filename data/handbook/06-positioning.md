## Definition

Positioning is the specific place your product occupies in a customer's mind relative to alternatives — the answer to "why this, instead of that (or instead of doing nothing)?"

## Why it matters

Customers compare, whether you tell them to or not. If you don't actively define your position, the market will assign you a generic one — usually "just another option," which competes mostly on price.

:::key-idea
Positioning isn't a slogan. It's a strategic choice about which single benefit you'll be known for, at the cost of not being "known for everything."
:::

## How it works

A classic positioning statement format:

> For [target audience], [product] is the [category] that [key benefit], unlike [main alternative], because [reason to believe].

This forces clarity on audience, category, benefit, competitor and proof — all in one sentence.

## Framework

1. List every realistic alternative a customer considers, including doing nothing.
2. Identify the one benefit that matters most to your target audience that alternatives don't deliver as well.
3. Write the positioning statement using the format above.
4. Pressure-test it: does it sound true, specific, and different — not just nice?

:::example
"For venues that already sell birthday packages, Sing My Birthday is the personalization add-on that turns a standard party into a keepsake moment, unlike generic decorations or photo props, because every song is written around that one child."
:::

## Common mistakes

- Trying to own multiple unrelated benefits at once (fast, cheap, premium, personal — pick one lead benefit).
- Positioning against every competitor at once instead of the most relevant one.
- Confusing a tagline with positioning — positioning is internal strategy; the tagline is one expression of it.

:::mistake
Copying the positioning language of the market leader. If you sound the same, you'll lose to them by default — they have more trust and budget.
:::

## Metrics to track

- Whether customers can repeat your key differentiator back in their own words
- Win rate against named competitors
- Price sensitivity (strong positioning reduces pure price-based objections)
