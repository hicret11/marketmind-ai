## Definition

Email marketing is direct, owned communication with people who've given you their email — used for nurturing leads, driving repeat purchases, and delivering content without depending on a platform's algorithm.

## Why it matters

Unlike social reach, an email list is owned — you control delivery and timing, and it typically produces some of the highest return on effort of any channel, especially for repeat purchases and abandoned-cart recovery.

:::key-idea
Email's power comes from timing and relevance (the right message at the right moment), not volume. A smaller, well-segmented list often outperforms a big generic one.
:::

## How it works

Core email types:

- **Broadcast/newsletter** — one-to-many updates, content or promotions.
- **Automated/lifecycle** — triggered by behavior (welcome series, abandoned cart, post-purchase).
- **Transactional** — receipts, confirmations — often under-used as a marketing surface too.

## Framework

1. Build the list ethically (clear opt-in, a real reason to subscribe).
2. Set up at least one automation: a welcome sequence for new subscribers/customers.
3. Segment by behavior (purchased vs. browsed vs. inactive) rather than sending one blast to everyone.
4. Test subject lines and send times — small changes here compound over volume.

:::example
A "your song is being written" + "your song is ready" two-email sequence turns a delivery wait into an engagement moment instead of silence.
:::

## Common mistakes

- Sending only when there's a promotion, training subscribers to ignore most emails.
- No segmentation — sending identical content to new leads and repeat customers.
- Neglecting mobile formatting, where most emails are actually opened.

:::mistake
Buying or scraping email lists — this destroys deliverability and violates most providers' policies and applicable law.
:::

## Metrics to track

- Open rate and click-through rate, by segment
- List growth rate and unsubscribe rate
- Revenue or conversions attributed to email specifically
