## Definition

Marketing is everything a business does to create, communicate and deliver value to the people it wants as customers — and to capture value back as sales, leads or attention. It sits between "we made something" and "someone wants it enough to act."

## Why it matters

Without marketing, even a great product goes unnoticed. Marketing turns a product into a business by connecting it to real demand: the right message, to the right audience, through the right channel, at the right time.

:::key-idea
Marketing is not advertising. Advertising is one tool inside marketing. Marketing also includes positioning, pricing, product decisions, distribution and retention.
:::

## How it works

Most marketing decisions come back to four questions, classically framed as the marketing mix:

- **Product** — what are you actually offering, and to whom?
- **Price** — what does it cost, and what does that price signal?
- **Place** — where and how do people discover and buy it?
- **Promotion** — how do you communicate the value clearly and repeatedly?

A weak answer to any one of these makes the others work harder than they should.

## Framework

1. Understand the customer (research, segmentation, audience).
2. Define the value proposition — the one sentence that says why someone should choose you.
3. Choose channels that match where that audience already pays attention.
4. Create messaging that ties the product directly to a real problem or desire.
5. Measure what actually happened, not what you hoped happened.

:::example
A children's party venue doesn't market "party packages." It markets "your child's birthday will feel like the best day of their year" — the outcome, not the feature list.
:::

## Common mistakes

- Leading with features instead of the outcome the customer actually wants.
- Marketing to "everyone" instead of a specific, describable buyer.
- Treating marketing as a one-time launch activity instead of an ongoing system.

:::mistake
Assuming that if the product is good, marketing will "take care of itself." Distribution and awareness are not automatic, no matter how good the product is.
:::

## Metrics to track

- Awareness (reach, impressions, branded search volume)
- Consideration (site visits, engagement, email signups)
- Conversion (purchases, bookings, qualified leads)

:::metric
Pick one metric per funnel stage you're trying to improve right now — not ten. Focus beats coverage in an early-stage marketing system.
:::
