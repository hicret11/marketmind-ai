## Definition

Marketing strategy is the set of choices about *who* you serve, *what* you promise them, and *how* you'll reach and convince them — made before any tactic (an ad, a post, an email) is chosen. Tactics without strategy is just activity.

## Why it matters

Most marketing failure isn't bad execution — it's no strategy, so every channel and campaign competes for attention with no shared logic. A clear strategy lets you say no to distracting tactics that don't serve the plan.

:::key-idea
Strategy is what you choose NOT to do. If your strategy doesn't rule anything out, it isn't a strategy yet — it's a wish list.
:::

## How it works

A workable marketing strategy answers five questions in order:

1. **Goal** — what business outcome are we driving (bookings, leads, revenue)?
2. **Audience** — who, specifically, do we need to reach to hit that goal?
3. **Positioning** — why should that audience pick us over the alternative (including "doing nothing")?
4. **Channels** — where does that audience already spend attention?
5. **Resources** — what can we realistically execute with the time/budget available?

## Framework

- Start from the business goal, not the channel ("we should be on TikTok" is not a strategy).
- Pick one primary audience segment to win before expanding to others.
- Write the positioning statement in one sentence a customer would actually say back to you.
- Choose 1–2 primary channels to go deep on rather than 5 channels done shallowly.
- Set a review cadence (monthly is usually right for an early-stage business) to check what's working.

:::example
A strategy for Sing My Birthday's B2B side might be: "Win 10 pilot partnerships with kids' party venues in one city within 90 days by pitching personalized songs as a premium add-on, measured by pilot bookings, not vanity reach."
:::

## Common mistakes

- Confusing a content calendar with a strategy.
- Chasing every new channel or trend instead of compounding on what's working.
- Setting goals with no way to measure them (e.g. "build brand awareness" with no defined signal).

:::mistake
Rewriting the strategy every time results are slow. Give a strategy a real testing window (usually 60–90 days) before declaring it wrong — most strategies fail from inconsistent execution, not bad logic.
:::

## Metrics to track

- Progress toward the single primary goal (not a dashboard of everything)
- Leading indicators specific to the chosen channels
- Cost or time invested vs. output, reviewed monthly
