## Definition

Content marketing is creating and sharing material — posts, videos, articles, guides — that provides value (useful, entertaining, or emotionally resonant) to a target audience, building trust and attention without directly asking for a sale every time.

## Why it matters

Paid channels get more expensive over time; content compounds. A well-performing piece keeps generating views, trust and traffic long after it's published, and it builds the brand equity that makes paid ads convert better too.

:::key-idea
Content marketing is a trust-building system, not a posting schedule. Consistency without a clear value angle just produces noise.
:::

## How it works

Effective content usually falls into one of a few buckets:

- **Educational** — teaches the audience something useful (how-to, tips).
- **Entertaining** — earns attention through humor, emotion or story.
- **Social proof** — shows real results, reviews, behind-the-scenes.
- **Product-in-context** — shows the product solving the real problem, not just describing it.

## Framework

1. Pick 2–3 content pillars tied directly to what your audience cares about (not just what you want to say).
2. Batch-create content around those pillars instead of scrambling daily.
3. Repurpose one strong piece across formats (a video becomes clips, a caption, a quote graphic).
4. Track which pillar and format actually drives engagement or leads — double down there.

:::example
For Sing My Birthday, a strong content pillar is "real reaction moments" — short videos of a child hearing their personalized song for the first time — emotional, shareable, and directly demonstrates the product.
:::

## Common mistakes

- Publishing purely promotional content with no value for the viewer.
- Inconsistent posting with no underlying content system or pillars.
- Measuring content by vanity metrics (likes) instead of business impact (leads, saves, shares that drive traffic).

:::mistake
Trying to be on every platform with the same content instead of adapting format and tone to where the content actually performs.
:::

## Metrics to track

- Engagement rate relative to reach (not just raw likes)
- Saves and shares (stronger buying-intent signals than likes)
- Content-attributed traffic and conversions
