## Definition

Retention marketing focuses on keeping existing customers engaged, satisfied and buying again — as opposed to acquisition marketing, which focuses on getting new customers in the door.

## Why it matters

Acquiring a new customer is typically far more expensive than retaining or reactivating an existing one. A business leaking customers after one purchase has to run acquisition harder just to stand still.

:::key-idea
Retention starts at the first purchase, not after it — a strong onboarding/delivery experience is the foundation of whether someone comes back.
:::

## How it works

Retention levers include:

- **Onboarding/delivery experience** — the first real experience of the product.
- **Follow-up communication** — check-ins, tips, or reminders tied to natural re-purchase triggers.
- **Loyalty/incentive** — rewards for repeat purchase or referral.
- **Reactivation** — targeted outreach to lapsed customers.

## Framework

1. Map natural re-purchase triggers (e.g. next year's birthday, a sibling's birthday).
2. Build a simple follow-up sequence tied to those triggers.
3. Ask for feedback and referrals shortly after a positive experience, while sentiment is high.
4. Track and specifically target lapsed customers instead of treating the list as static.

:::example
Sing My Birthday could send a reminder email roughly 11 months after a purchase — "next year's birthday is coming up" — tied directly to a natural, high-relevance repurchase trigger.
:::

## Common mistakes

- Spending 100% of budget on acquisition with no retention system for existing customers.
- Asking for a referral or review before the customer has had a good experience.
- Treating every past customer identically instead of segmenting by recency/engagement.

:::mistake
Assuming a one-time-purchase product "doesn't need retention marketing" — referrals, gifting occasions and repeat life events (another birthday, another child) are still retention opportunities.
:::

## Metrics to track

- Repeat purchase rate and average time between purchases
- Customer referral rate
- Churn / lapse rate among past customers
