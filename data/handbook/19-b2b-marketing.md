## Definition

B2B (business-to-business) marketing targets organizations — venues, agencies, retailers, partners — rather than individual consumers, typically involving a longer sales cycle, multiple stakeholders, and a more evidence-driven decision process.

## Why it matters

B2B buyers evaluate risk and ROI more explicitly than B2C consumers — they're often spending someone else's budget (or their business's) and need to justify the decision internally. Marketing that only sells on emotion, without practical proof, underperforms in B2B.

:::key-idea
B2B marketing sells a business outcome (more revenue, less risk, easier operations) — the emotional/lifestyle angle that works in B2C usually needs to be reframed as a business benefit.
:::

## How it works

Key B2B differences from B2C:

- **Decision-maker vs. user** — the person you pitch (owner, manager) may not be the end user.
- **Longer cycle** — often multiple touches (content, calls, pilot) before a decision.
- **Proof-driven** — case studies, pilots and specifics outperform broad claims.
- **Relationship-based** — trust and responsiveness matter heavily, especially for smaller businesses.

## Framework

1. Identify the actual decision-maker role for your product (owner vs. events manager vs. marketing lead).
2. Lead outreach with a specific, evidenced observation about their business — not a generic pitch.
3. Offer a low-risk entry point (a pilot, a trial period) rather than asking for a full commitment upfront.
4. Follow up with concrete next steps and a clear, simple proposal.

:::example
Pitching a venue: "You already offer birthday packages with a party host — a personalized song could be a premium $X add-on with no operational lift on your side. Want to pilot it with your next 10 bookings?" — specific, low-risk, and framed as a business upside.
:::

## Common mistakes

- Using consumer-style emotional ad creative with no business-outcome framing for B2B prospects.
- Pitching the wrong role (e.g. a general manager instead of the events coordinator who actually owns the decision).
- Asking for a big commitment before establishing any trust or proof.

:::mistake
Treating every business the same way regardless of size — a large chain and a single-location owner have very different decision processes and risk tolerance.
:::

## Metrics to track

- Number of qualified conversations with real decision-makers
- Pilot-to-full-partnership conversion rate
- Average sales cycle length
