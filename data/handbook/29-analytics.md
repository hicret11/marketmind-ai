## Definition

Marketing analytics is the practice of collecting, organizing and interpreting data about marketing performance — traffic, engagement, conversions, revenue — to make informed decisions instead of relying on intuition alone.

## Why it matters

Without reliable analytics, you can't tell which channels, campaigns or pages are actually working, which leads to either wasted spend on what isn't working or under-investment in what is.

:::key-idea
The goal of analytics isn't more data — it's fewer, better decisions. A small dashboard tied to real decisions beats a huge one nobody acts on.
:::

## How it works

A useful analytics setup usually covers:

- **Traffic/reach** — where visibility is coming from.
- **Behavior** — what people do once they arrive (pages viewed, time on page, drop-off).
- **Conversion** — the actions that matter to the business (leads, sales, bookings).
- **Attribution** — a reasonable (even if imperfect) view of which channel/touch drove the result.

## Framework

1. Define the handful of metrics that actually drive decisions before building a dashboard.
2. Set up tracking for the full funnel, not just top-of-funnel traffic.
3. Review on a fixed cadence (weekly for fast-moving campaigns, monthly for strategy).
4. Always ask "what would we do differently based on this number?" — if nothing, it's not a decision-driving metric.

:::example
Tracking "cost per booked pilot call" end-to-end (from ad spend through to a booked call) is far more decision-useful than tracking ad impressions alone.
:::

## Common mistakes

- Tracking vanity metrics (impressions, followers) as if they were business results.
- No clear attribution model, making every channel claim credit for the same conversions.
- Building dashboards nobody actually reviews or acts on.

:::mistake
Over-relying on last-click attribution, which over-credits the final touchpoint (often retargeting) and under-credits the channels that built awareness earlier in the journey.
:::

## Metrics to track

- A small set of decision-driving KPIs (see Marketing KPIs)
- Funnel conversion rates end-to-end
- Data quality/tracking accuracy itself (broken tracking silently ruins every other number)
